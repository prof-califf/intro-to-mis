Lab 4 · Week 4

Data Visualization: Communicating Status

Lab session · 80 minPairs with Chapter 4 · Systems Analysis →

Healthcare.gov failed in part because nobody built the picture that would have made the truth undeniable. A chart is not decoration. It is how a system's status gets communicated to the people who decide. This week you build Cascadia's status pictures, and you build them honestly.

Work from your cleaned Lab 3 file, or re-download the clean workbook.

From lecture to lab

Lab professor's intro (10 min): Connect to Monday's Healthcare.gov case: the site's builders had no dashboard that told leadership, honestly, whether the system was ready: status was communicated in meetings, optimistically. This lab is about charts as honest status instruments. Students pivot the Cascadia data and build a small dashboard: monthly revenue line, category bar, store comparison. Emphasize chart-type choice (time = line, comparison = bar) and axis honesty. Difficulty is moderate; pivots were previewed in DataCamp's Visualization module.

SkillsPivot tables, line charts, bar charts, dashboard assembly

  1. Monthly revenue, as a line. Pivot with sale_date grouped by month against revenue; insert a line chart. Seasonality should jump out. This is a gear co-op, and the calendar is its heartbeat.
  2. Category revenue, as bars. New pivot, categories sorted descending, bar chart. Resist 3-D. Resist pie.
  3. Store comparison. Revenue by store, bars, sorted. Title it as a claim, not a label: "Seattle Flagship carries a fifth of the co-op" beats "Revenue by Store."
  4. Assemble. Arrange the three charts on one sheet named Dashboard so a manager gets the year in one glance.
Axis honesty

Truncating a bar chart's y-axis manufactures drama the data doesn't contain. Healthcare.gov's leadership got optimistic pictures for three years; the correction arrived all at once on launch day. Your dashboards should make bad news visible early, because that is what dashboards are for.

What you have not been asked to chart

Notice this dashboard says nothing about members. Revenue looks healthy everywhere. In Lab 5, after the exam, the co-op's board asks a question this dashboard cannot answer, and you will find that healthy revenue can hide a sick store.
