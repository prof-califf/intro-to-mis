SkillsSELECT · WHERE · aggregates · GROUP BY · JOIN · NULL

  1. Start by counting things. Start by counting rows in each table: get a feel for the database's shape before querying it.
  2. WHERE. Filter: one category, one store, one date range. Strings take single quotes; dates here are text in YYYY-MM-DD form, so they compare correctly.
  3. Aggregates. SUM, AVG, MIN, MAX: reproduce your Lab 2 totals in single lines.
  4. GROUP BY. Revenue by category, by store, by month (substr(sale_date,6,2) extracts the month). Every pivot table you built this quarter is a GROUP BY wearing a costume.
  5. JOIN. The IDs in sales point at other tables; JOIN follows the pointer. Names live in stores and products, not in sales. That separation is the point of Monday's normalization lecture.
  6. NULL. A missing member_id is not zero and not empty text: it's NULL, and it takes IS NULL / IS NOT NULL. The co-op's biggest problem lives in exactly those rows.
