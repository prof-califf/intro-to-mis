Optional · Extra credit

Excel for Business: Lookups, Pivots, and Power Query

About 3 to 5 hours, at your own paceBuilds on Labs 2 to 5 →

Labs 2 to 5 taught the Excel most people use: formulas, charts, and a first analysis. This bootcamp is the Excel that gets you trusted with the real workbook. You build Tables that grow on their own, lookups that pull data from one table into another, sums with conditions, and formulas that return whole lists. Then come PivotTables, a model you can run backward, and Power Query to clean a messy export once and for all. It uses the same Cascadia sales, in a workbook made for it.

WhyWhy more Excel, when you have SQL and Python

Because it is where the business already works. Budgets, forecasts, price lists, and the export someone sends you on Monday morning arrive as spreadsheets, and the people who will read your answer open them in Excel. SQL and Python are better for big or repeated jobs. Excel is where most decisions are argued over.

The gap between basic and strong Excel is mostly in this bootcamp. Lookups join two tables, the same idea as a JOIN in Lab 7. SUMIFS and PivotTables answer most "how much, where, and when" questions in a minute. Dynamic arrays let one formula return a whole list. Power Query turns an hour of hand cleaning into steps that rerun with one click, which is Chapter 4's point about processes: do it the same way every time.

Where you would use it

Finance: pull this month's actuals next to the budget with a lookup. Sales: a PivotTable of bookings by rep and region, with a slicer for the quarter. Operations: clean the weekly export from the warehouse system with Power Query. MIS: check that a new system brought over every record from the old one. Business analytics: a first-cut forecast to test an idea before anyone builds a model. Any role: a small model with inputs a manager can change.

Who this is for, and what you get

It is optional. If the Excel labs went well and you want the skills that show up in analyst interviews, this is it. You learn in 26 short steps, one idea at a time, and you build every formula yourself. Pass every step, write a short memo, and email your certificate to Professor Califf (califfc@wwu.edu). Each bootcamp you finish adds 2 points to the exam of your choice; say which exam in your email. Do it in several sittings; your progress saves in this browser.

For a resume: Completed a 26-step advanced Excel bootcamp: Tables, XLOOKUP, SUMIFS, dynamic arrays, PivotTables, Goal Seek, and Power Query on a 2,899-row retail dataset. The resume page shows where it goes.

Green · AI as coach

Ask an AI what an error like #SPILL! or #N/A means, or where a menu lives on your version of Excel. Then fix it yourself. Do not ask it to write the formula for a step. Writing formulas is the skill, and the certificate says you have it.

Before you start

Download cascadia-excel-bootcamp.xlsx and save it somewhere you can find it. Do every step in that one workbook: later steps use the columns you add in earlier ones.

Use the desktop Excel app, version Microsoft 365 or Excel 2021 or later, on Windows or Mac. Older versions do not have XLOOKUP, FILTER, or UNIQUE.

How the steps work

Each step teaches one small idea, then gives you a task. Do the task in Excel, then paste the formula you wrote and type the result Excel shows, and press Check. If both are right, the next step opens. If not, you get a note on what to fix. Every step has a hint, and after two tries you can see a solution. There is no penalty for wrong tries.

Your progress saves in this browser, so you can stop and come back. Your work itself lives in your Excel file, so save it often. To start over, use the button.
