Lab 2 · Week 2

Excel Basics: Meeting the Client

Lab session · 80 minPairs with Chapter 2 · The Value of Information →

Meet your client. Cascadia Outfitters is a member-owned gear co-op with eight stores from Boise to Bellingham, and for the next month you are their analyst. Here is the thing to understand about that job on day one: analysts do not "do formulas." They answer questions that arrive from real people, usually by email, usually needing numbers. Two such emails arrive in this lab. Part A solves the first one together, every click and keystroke shown. Part B hands you the second email to answer alone, with the same moves. If you have never used Excel, you cannot get lost; if you have, the emails are still worth answering well.

Download

cascadia-sales-2025.xlsx, one sheet, one year of sales. Columns you will use today: C store, H quantity, K revenue.

Red · No AI in Labs 2 through 5

AI use is not allowed in these four labs. That means no formula help, no error explanations, and no AI checking your work. An AI tool can produce accurate responses to this lab in seconds, which is exactly why it is not allowed: the AI's work never becomes your own.

These four labs build your Excel skills, which are critical in business settings. So, even though it is tedious, this means typing every formula yourself. The goal is to be able to write a formula like =SUM without thinking. If you get stuck, that is what the walkthrough steps, the hints, and your lab professor are for.

AI comes back in Lab 6, where you will use it as a coach under the three lights from Lab 1.

Email 1 · Monday, 9:04 AM

From: General Manager · Subject: Numbers for the member newsletter

"Welcome aboard. The January newsletter goes to print Thursday and I want to open it with our year in three numbers: how many sales we rang up in 2025, what they added up to, and what a typical sale looked like. Can you pull those from the sales export? Exact figures please, the members read closely."

How the checkpoints work

Here is how this works. Each checkpoint sits directly under the step it belongs to. You do the actual work in your own file, then paste the answer here. Get it right and the next question opens up. There is no penalty for a wrong answer, so guess, check, and lean on the hint if you get stuck. That is what it is there for.

Once you have cleared them all, write your reflection and download your certificate for Canvas. Your progress saves automatically in this browser, so you can close the tab and pick up where you left off. It does not follow you to a different computer or a private window, and clearing your browser data clears it. If you need to start this lab over, use the button.

Part AFollow along: answering the GM, together

Her three numbers are a count, a total, and an average. Do these steps in order, exactly as written; Checkpoints 1 through 3 are her three answers.

Step 1Save it properly

Open the download, then File → Save As, and name it LAB2_Last_First.xlsx. You will submit a file like this every week, so start the naming habit now. The workbook opens on a tab called Analyst. That is where every answer goes: checkpoint 1 in cell B5, checkpoint 2 in B6, and so on down the numbered rows. The sales data is on the Sales2025 tab next to it.

Step 2Make it readable

Click the triangle above row 1 and left of column A to select everything, then double-click any boundary between two column letters; every column snaps to fit. Then View tab → Freeze Panes → Freeze Top Row, so the headers stay put while you scroll.

Step 3Her first number: how many salesAnswer goes in Checkpoint 1 · cell B5

Press Ctrl+End (Mac Cmd+Fn+→ then ↓) to jump to the data's last corner and read the row number. The header row takes one of those rows, so the number of sales is that figure minus one. Type that number on the Analyst tab in B5.

Step 4Her second number: total revenueAnswer goes in Checkpoint 2 · cell B6

Go to the Analyst tab and click cell B6. Type =SUM(Sales2025!K:K) exactly, and press Enter. Read the formula left to right: add up everything in column K of the Sales2025 tab. The Sales2025! part is how a formula on one tab reaches data on another; you will use it all quarter.

Step 5Her third number: the typical saleAnswer goes in Checkpoint 3 · cell B7

In B7 on the Analyst tab, type =AVERAGE(Sales2025!K:K). Same three parts as Step 4; the only change is what Excel does with the column, averaging it instead of adding it.

Step 6Check yourself with the status bar

Click column K's header letter to select the whole column, then look at the bar along the bottom of the window: Excel shows Average, Count, and Sum for any selection automatically. All three of the GM's numbers are sitting there, confirming your formulas (the Count includes nothing extra because column K has one header cell and no stray values). Remember this; Part B uses it.

Step 7Write the reply

Your Analyst tab now holds three answers in B5:B7. That block, tidy, numbered, checkable, is the actual deliverable of analyst work, and every lab this month ends with one. In the Notes column beside each answer, write one short line saying what the number is, the way you would if the GM opened this file. Her email is answered.

If a step misbehaves
  1. Freeze Panes is grayed out: you are in a cell-editing state; press Esc first.
  2. =SUM shows 0: you are probably on a different sheet, or typed a letter other than K. Click the cell and read the formula bar.
  3. #NAME? error: the function name is misspelled. Delete and retype.
  4. The status bar shows nothing: right-click the status bar itself and check Sum, Average, and Count.
Email 2 · Monday, 11:40 AM

From: Operations Manager · Subject: Three quick ones before Thursday

"Heard you pulled the newsletter numbers, so you get my list too. One: the warehouse recount is Thursday and I need to know how many total units we moved in 2025. Two: I am setting winter staffing levels and need to know which store produced the most revenue. Three: our payment processor flagged our largest single transaction of the year for review; how much was it? Same drill, exact figures."

Part BNow you: the ops email, alone

Everything he asks for is a move you made in Part A, pointed at a new target. Steps 8 through 10 each tell you exactly what to do; doing it with your own hands is the point. Build his answers as a labeled block, the Step 7 habit.

Step 8Ops email: total units movedAnswer goes in Checkpoint 4

Click an empty cell and write a SUM formula on the quantity column, which is column H, the same way you wrote Step 4's formula on column K. Label it.

Step 9Ops email: the leading storeAnswer goes in Checkpoint 5

Put a filter on (click any cell in the data, Data tab → Filter), use column C's dropdown to show one store at a time, and read column K's Sum in the status bar, the Step 6 trick. Work through the stores until you are sure, then clear the filter and record the winner.

Step 10Ops email: the flagged transactionAnswer goes in Checkpoint 6

Click another empty cell and write a MAX formula on the revenue column (K). MAX works exactly like SUM: one column in, one number out. If you want to see the actual row, sort by revenue, largest to smallest.

Why a co-op cares about members (a seed for later)

Column N marks whether each sale had a member attached. Every "N" is a customer the co-op earned nothing durable from: no relationship, no dividend, no reason to come back. Neither email asked about it today. Scroll past it once and remember it exists; an email about that column is coming later this month, and it will matter more than revenue.

Finished early? One stretch

Select the whole data range and Insert → Table (Ctrl+T). Notice the filter arrows appear on their own and formulas start referring to columns by name. Then just explore: filter to your favorite store, read a week of its sales. Familiarity with a dataset is part of analysis, not a delay before it.
