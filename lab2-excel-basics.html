Lab 2 · Week 2

Excel Basics: Meeting the Client

Lab session · 80 minPairs with Chapter 1 · The Value of Information →

Meet your client. Cascadia Outfitters is a member-owned gear co-op with eight stores from Boise to Bellingham, 35 products, and a business model that lives or dies on its members. For the next month, you are their analyst.

Today is the gentle one, and that is deliberate. Before you can find anything interesting in a company's data, you have to know what is actually in it. Every analyst you will ever work with starts a new engagement exactly this way.

Download cascadia-sales-2025.xlsx, and read the Read Me tab before you touch anything else. I know that sounds like routine advice. Analysts who skip the data dictionary are the ones who confidently present the wrong number.

From lecture to lab

Lab professor's intro (10 min): Introduce the client: Cascadia Outfitters, a member-owned outdoor co-op with eight PNW stores, whose 2025 sales data the class will work in Excel, Tableau, and SQL all quarter. Show the workbook's Read Me tab on the projector and define member attach, a sale linked to a member number, because the co-op's dividend and demand planning both depend on it. Then let them work. This lab is deliberately gentle; DataCamp's Introduction to Excel is its companion. Do not preview later labs' findings.

Green · AI as coach

Stuck on what SUMIF does or why a formula errors? Ask an AI to explain it, then write the formula yourself. Do not paste the checkpoint questions in and ask for answers; the checkpoint measures whether your workbook is right, and the workbook is yours.

SkillsNavigation, SUM, AVERAGE, sort, filter, SUMIF

  1. Get oriented first. Press Ctrl+End (Cmd+Fn+Right on a Mac) to jump to the far corner of the data. Now you know how big the dataset is. Scroll around a bit. I have already frozen the header row for you, so watch what that does as you go down.
  2. Get your totals. Find some empty space to the right of the data and build yourself a scratch area. Total revenue, total units, average revenue per line, using SUM and AVERAGE. Keep these visible, because you will be checking later work against them.
  3. Poke around. Sort by revenue, highest first. What is the single biggest sale of the year, and does it surprise you? Then filter down to whichever store you like best and read a week of its sales. Building familiarity with a dataset is part of the analysis, not a delay before it.
    4. How to make a pivot table
    1. Click any single cell inside the data.
    2. Insert tab, then PivotTable. Mac same menu, but the button may read Summarize with PivotTable.
    3. Excel guesses the data range. Check that it covers all the rows, then choose New Worksheet and click OK.
    4. A panel appears on the right with your column names. Drag a field into Rows to group by it, and drag a number into Values to total it.
    5. If Values shows a count instead of a sum, click it, choose Value Field Settings, and pick Sum. This catches almost everyone once.

    If your pivot is empty: you probably selected a single cell outside the data before inserting. Undo and click inside the table first.

  4. Now make Excel answer a question. SUMIF revenue by store name. COUNTIF rows where is_member is "N": you'll meet that number again later in the course.
Why a co-op cares about attach

Every sale without a member number is a customer the co-op cannot send a dividend to, cannot survey, and cannot see in its demand planning. Keep that in the back of your mind. Just the back, for now.

When you get stuck

Before raising your hand, try these in order. Most problems in this lab are one of them.

  1. Re-read the checkpoint question. Is it asking for a count, a total, or an average? Those are three different numbers.
  2. Check whether your number is plausible. If total revenue comes out as 12 or as 40 million, something is off by a lot and you can usually see where.
  3. Expand the grey "How to" box for that step. It has the exact clicks.
  4. Check the formula bar of the cell you think is wrong. Does it reference the range you meant?
  5. Use the hint button on the checkpoint. It is not a penalty, it is part of the lab.
  6. Ask a neighbour. Then raise your hand.
