Tableau Bootcamp: From Charts to Analysis

About 3 to 5 hours, at your own paceBuilds on Lab 6 →

Lab 6 got you from a CSV file to a working dashboard in one session. That is the start of Tableau. Analysts spend most of their time on what comes next: calculations that answer questions the raw columns cannot, comparisons across time, numbers fixed at the right level of detail, and views a manager can adjust without calling you. This bootcamp covers those, on the same Cascadia data, and ends with a dashboard you publish and can link from a resume.

WhyWhy Tableau: an answer nobody sees changes nothing

Most of the people who make decisions will never open your spreadsheet or read your query. They will look at a chart, briefly, between meetings. Tableau, and tools like it such as Power BI, exist so the person who found the answer can hand it to the people who decide, in a form they can explore themselves: filter to their region, change the top ten to the top twenty, look at their month.

Lab 6 covered the mechanics. The skills in this bootcamp are what separate a chart from an analysis: calculations that answer a real question, comparisons over time, and numbers fixed at the right level of detail, which is Chapter 6's lesson about measuring the right thing. A chart can be accurate and still answer the wrong question.

It is also the one bootcamp that leaves you with something to show. A published dashboard is a portfolio piece an interviewer can click, and it shows what you can do faster than a line on a resume.

Where you would use it

Sales: a weekly pipeline view each manager filters to their own team. Operations: a store scorecard leadership checks every Monday. Marketing: campaign results by channel and month. Nonprofits and government: public dashboards that let anyone check the numbers.

1Dates: the same sales, cut by time

About 30 minutes. Tableau reads sale_date as a date, which means it can roll the 2,899 sales up to any level of time. Drag sale_date to Columns and revenue to Rows. You get one bar per year, and there is only one year. Click the small plus sign on the date pill to drill down to quarters.

A date can be used two ways. Date parts (blue pills) group by the label, so every January lands together. Date values (green pills) keep the calendar order. Right-click the pill to switch. For this module, date parts are what you want.

2Calculated fields: columns you invent

About 40 minutes. In Lab 6 you built one calculated field, the margin rate. Here is the rule that makes those work: aggregate before you divide. SUM([gross_margin]) / SUM([revenue]) gives the margin rate for whatever the view groups by. Writing [gross_margin] / [revenue] divides row by row and then adds up the percentages, which is a different and usually wrong number.

Rebuild margin_rate (Analysis → Create Calculated Field), format it as a percentage, and show it by supplier.

Module 2Margin by supplierAnswer goes in Checkpoint 3

Calculated fields can also label rows. Create Order size:

IF [quantity] >= 3 THEN "Bulk" ELSE "Single" END

Drag it to Color on a single bar of total revenue, then right-click the revenue pill and choose Quick Table Calculation → Percent of Total.

Module 2A share by labelAnswer goes in Checkpoint 4

3Table calculations: numbers that depend on other rows

About 45 minutes. A table calculation is computed from the numbers already in the view, after Tableau has added them up. You just used one, Percent of Total. Three more cover most of what managers ask for: share of the whole, the total so far, and change from last period.

Share. Show revenue by category as a bar chart, then apply Percent of Total.

Module 3Share of the wholeAnswer goes in Checkpoint 5

Total so far. Show revenue by month as a line (use the MONTH date part). Right-click the revenue pill and choose Quick Table Calculation → Running Total. Each point now shows revenue from January up to that month.

Module 3Running totalAnswer goes in Checkpoint 6

Change from last period. Undo the running total and choose Percent Difference instead. Each month now shows its change from the month before, as a percentage. Switch to bars so the drops stand out.

Module 3Month over monthAnswer goes in Checkpoint 7

4Level of detail: fixing a number at the right level

About 45 minutes. Every view has a level of detail: whatever is on Rows, Columns, and the Marks card decides what Tableau adds up. Sometimes you need a number at a different level than the view. "How much did each member spend all year?" is a question about members, even when the view shows stores. A FIXED level-of-detail expression computes at the level you name and ignores the view:

{FIXED [member_id] : SUM([revenue])}

Name it Member spend. Right-click it in the Data pane and choose Convert to Dimension, so Tableau treats each member's total as a value you can filter on. Then put member_id on Rows and filter Member spend to the range you need. The bottom of the window tells you how many rows are showing.

Module 4Spend per memberAnswer goes in Checkpoint 8

The same trick finds each member's first purchase, the start of every cohort analysis:

{FIXED [member_id] : MIN([sale_date])}

Put the month of that field on Columns and COUNTD([member_id]) on Rows. Sales with no member attached show up as a blank member_id; exclude the Null.

Module 4First purchasesAnswer goes in Checkpoint 9

5Parameters and sets: views a manager can steer

About 40 minutes. A parameter is a value the viewer controls. Create one called Top N: an integer, current value 5. Then show revenue by product, drag product to Filters, open the Top tab, choose By field, and pick Top N by SUM of revenue. Right-click the parameter and Show Parameter. Now a manager can type 3 or 15 and the chart follows.

Module 5Top N productsAnswer goes in Checkpoint 10

A set splits a dimension into In and Out. Right-click product in the Data pane, choose Create → Set, and on the Top tab keep the top 10 by SUM(revenue). Make a single bar of revenue, put the set on Color, and apply Percent of Total. The question it answers is one every retailer asks: how much of the business rides on a few products?

Module 5The top tenAnswer goes in Checkpoint 11

CapstonePublish a dashboard you would show an employer

About 45 minutes. Build one dashboard, for the co-op's board, that answers a single question you choose. Good ones from this data: Which products carry the business, and are they the profitable ones? Where is membership strong or weak? What changed month to month, and where? Use at least one calculated field, one table calculation or level-of-detail expression, and one filter or parameter a viewer can use. Give it a title that states the finding, not the topic.

Then publish it: in Tableau Public, File → Save to Tableau Public. Copy the link. It goes in your memo.

Four plain rules for any dashboard

Bars start at zero. One idea per chart. Color means something or it is gray. Label the number the reader needs, not every number.
