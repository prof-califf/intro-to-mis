Lab 6 · Week 6

Tableau — Seeing the Co-op

Lab session · 80 minPairs with Chapter 7 · BI & Analytics (Moneyball)

Same client, new instrument. Everything you found in Excel is still true — but this week you'll watch a finding that took a careful pivot table become impossible to miss the moment it's drawn. That difference is why analytics teams fight over dashboards.

Download cascadia-sales-2025.csv and connect Tableau (or free Tableau Public) to it.

From lecture to lab

Lab professor's intro (10 min): Frame with Monday's Moneyball discussion: the A's won by measuring what the league didn't. Today students point a new instrument — Tableau — at data they already know, and re-find last week's discovery visually, which lands the argument for visualization better than any lecture could. Show connecting to a CSV on the projector (Data Source pane, then one drag-and-drop), then release them. If desktop Tableau isn't installed, Tableau Public is free and sufficient. Checkpoint 5's answer is deliberately counterintuitive — let them be surprised.

SkillsConnecting data, marks & shelves, calculated fields, highlight tables

  1. Connect and inspect. Open the CSV as a data source; check the row count and that Tableau typed revenue as a number and sale_date as a date.
  2. First chart in 60 seconds. Revenue by store as sorted bars. Then swap store for category, then for product — feel how fast iteration is compared to rebuilding a pivot.
  3. Calculated fields. Build margin_rate and format it as a percent. This is the Moneyball move: a ratio the raw data doesn't contain, made first-class.
  4. Highlight table. Store by month, colored by revenue — the co-op's year on one screen. Find the surprises: the best single cell is not in the best month.
  5. The re-discovery. Build attach rate by store and watch last week's investigation become a single picture. Screenshot this one — it goes in your submission.
Undervalued gear

Before you leave: put revenue on one axis and margin_rate on the other, by category. The A's looked for players the market underpriced; a merchant looks for categories earning a high rate on low volume. Which category would you tell Cascadia to promote harder? No checkpoint for this — your pick and one sentence of reasoning go in the submission note, and there is more than one defensible answer.
